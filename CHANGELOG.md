# Changelog

## 1.8.4

- **Detect metal hardware** ignores materials the garment's main piece also uses. Imports often give every material Metallic 1, which made every strap and patch rigid "hardware": brushes moved them as stiff pieces that stayed straight and slid off the fabric, and the weight fit gave them shared rigid weights (supplied tech pants: 137 → 112 detected pieces, all 0.7–5 cm buckles and rings; the 25 straps and patches now bend with the fabric, following it within about 3 mm on a 15 cm pull).
- Brush symmetry finds the mirrored spot more reliably: it casts the mirrored view ray as well as taking the nearest surface within the brush radius (before: nearest within 0.3 radius) and prefers the same connected piece as the original. With Topology off a mirrored pass is never dropped; without surface there it still works around the mirrored point, as Blender's symmetry does. On 326 random spots on the supplied pants, strokes on the main fabric lost their mirror pass 53 times and mirrored onto another piece 13 times; now 0 and 7.

## 1.8.3

- With Topology automasking off, Cloth Fit brushes now move hardware (marked hardware and small detached metal pieces such as rings and buckles) directly, like any other vertex in reach, and keep it rigid (best rigid fit to the brushed motion). Before, hardware only followed the nearest fabric, so a buckle bound to the pants under its strap or a ring hanging away from the fabric was left behind. With Topology on, hardware still follows the fabric it sits on.

## 1.8.2

- Cloth Fit brushes follow Sculpt Mode's **Topology** automasking (toggle also in the tool header): on, a brush reaches only the connected part under the cursor along the surface (the 1.8.1 behavior); off, like Blender's Elastic Grab, it reaches every vertex within its radius by straight-line distance, including separate parts and nearby layers.

## 1.8.1

- Cloth Fit brushes now work on the shape you see when sculpting the Cloth Fit Assisted key: the Basis plus every active shape key (vertex-group-limited keys included), not the sculpted key alone. Before, a garment with a **Cloth Fit Correction** key (from a weight fit) was brushed on its hidden uncorrected surface: Shape Relax saw no lumps to remove and did nothing (supplied hoodie: correction offsets up to 10 mm across 10,603 vertices), Fit to Body measured the gap from the wrong surface, and strokes hit up to the correction's offset away from the cursor. Changes are stored in the sculpted key only (divided by its value, as Adjust Region does); a muted or zero-value sculpted key is reported instead of silently editing nothing.

## 1.8.0

- Smoother Fit to Body and Shape Relax strokes: only the newest queued mouse move is handled (Blender marks the older ones as in-between; 1.7 ran a dab for each and fell behind), dabs are spaced along the path since the last one (at most three per move, with the strength of the skipped ones) and the mesh is written once per move. Shape Relax dabs are about 4× cheaper (48 → 12 ms for ~1000 brushed vertices on the supplied fishnet) and Fit to Body dabs about 1.5× (normals only around the brush, seam spreading only when a seam exists, faster body queries).
- Smart Grab now behaves like Blender's Elastic Grab, made for clothing: a regularized Kelvinlet fade along the fabric (about half strength at the brush circle, a soft tail out to three radii) instead of a short falloff solved as-rigid-as-possible around a tiny grabbed spot. The result depends only on the mouse position (dragging back restores the fabric, no jump on release); **Shape preservation** (default 0.6) adds an as-rigid-as-possible solve within 1.5 radii with soft pulls toward the elastic target. On the supplied garments a 7 cm pull flips no triangles (1.7.1: 11–88) at a third or less of the average edge strain. The Falloff setting no longer applies to Smart Grab.
- Shape Relax no longer sinks skin-tight clothing into the body: it smooths only the difference from the size-matched reference, so fabric pushed out by Fit to Body or Smart Grab stays out (a 4 mm push on a skin-tight sleeve keeps ≥ 3.4 mm over 30 dabs; 1.7.1 kept about 3 mm), and with a Body set it never pulls fabric closer to the skin than the Clearance or than it already was (supplied fishnet: 1.2% of brushed vertices ended inside the body with 1.7.1, none now). Clearance and Reach are shown for Shape Relax when a Body is set.
- The status, fit report (poses checked, library poses, warnings), outfit list and panel hints are collapsible sections, closed by default; their header keeps a one-line summary. Progress stays visible while a fit runs.

## 1.7.1

- Sculpt tools no longer refuse a rest-pose avatar because of import rounding (bone offsets around 0.01 mm, scales like 0.999999). A rig now counts as posed only when a deform bone visibly differs from rest in its evaluated pose (more than 0.5°, 0.1% of the rig size or 0.5% scale), and the message names that bone and how far it is off.

## 1.4.0

- **Auto Fit shape (new, off by default).** A separate geometry-fitting stage reshapes the clothing to the avatar before weight transfer, in the avatar's rest pose, and stores the result as a **Cloth Fit Auto Fit** shape key relative to the Basis. Topology, UVs, materials, vertex groups, the Basis and every other key stay unchanged. The rest of the pipeline then runs on the fitted shape: body correspondence, weight transfer, pose screening and refinement are all recomputed from it. New module `autofit.py`.
- **Coarse to fine.** (1) A separate clothing armature whose rest bones differ from the avatar's is treated as the garment's source skeleton and retargeted bone by bone (rotation, roll, length) by linear blend skinning with the garment's own weights. (2) Garment vertices are sliced into cross-section rings along the dominant avatar bone under them; rings that surround their own centre (not discs) wrap a limb or the torso, and the body's cross-section centre and radius along each bone come from the vertices that bone dominates. (3) A garment is treated as proportionally oversized only when the tightest tenth (by area) of its wrapped cross sections is still more than 12% larger than the body plus a modest ease; it is then scaled uniformly toward the body. Garments resting on the body somewhere keep every looser region. (4) A bone's garment segment that penetrates the body while the body's centre lies well off the ring centre is recentred, together with the rest of the limb beyond it; one rigid motion per bone fitted to the ring centres follows the limb's curved centreline (straight legs bend onto digitigrade legs). (5) An as-rigid-as-possible solve on a control graph of surface clusters (bounded local scale 0.82–1.22) follows those targets, resolves body penetration on coarse collision probes, keeps stacked layers and attached parts coupled, and carries folds and hanging fabric rigidly. (6) A fine stage pushes out remaining vertex, face and edge contacts with surface-smoothed, strain-limited updates in a geodesic halo around them, keeps facing garment surfaces (two legs, fold layers) at least half their rest gap apart, separates new self-intersections, and stops pushing contacts that cannot be resolved without tearing.
- **Looseness.** Ring ease (the near-body percentile of the gap) separates design looseness from contact; drape above it follows rigidity rather than targets. **Fit looseness** scales the cross-section ease (1 keeps the design, 0 fits snugly); penetration is always resolved.
- **Parts.** Hardware islands (marked or metallic) stay rigid; straps, patches and pockets are coupled to the fabric they rest on and may bend; small islands near nothing are reported as ambiguous and only follow the proportional scale. User part marks are honoured, never reclassified.
- **Acceptance.** Penetration, flipped/collapsed/folded faces, torn (>160%) or crushed (<45%) edges, distorted-edge fraction, new garment self-intersections and opening perimeters are measured against the original. The most complete stage (fine, deformation, alignment, proportion) without severe regressions is kept, otherwise the original shape; nothing is written when nothing changes. Unresolved contacts are reported with their cause (deformation limit, local stretch beyond the bound, ambiguous contact). Report key `auto_fit` holds the outcome, intent (`fitted`, `intentionally loose`, `oversized`, `too small or misaligned`), global scale, components, per-stage quality, rejected stages and seconds per phase; `phase_seconds.auto_fit` is added.
- New settings **Auto Fit shape**, **Fit looseness** and **Max deformation (% avatar size)** (0 = automatic, 20%).
- Reruns no longer stack fit keys: the Auto Fit key and the **Cloth Fit Correction** key are reused and overwritten, computed from the shape without them. A stale correction key is disabled (value 0, reported) when Auto Fit changes the shape. Restore, cancellation and rollback cover both keys.
- `ContactSamples` moved to `geometry.py` (still importable from `engine`).
- Add `tests/autofit_regression.py` and `tests/autofit_scenes.py`: tight clothing, an oversized hoodie, baggy tech pants with a pocket, irregular triangulation, folded fabric, floating accessories, human pants on a digitigrade avatar, a garment rigged for another body, a too-small garment with a deformation limit, a garment far from the body, shape-key storage/rerun/restore/cancellation, and weight transfer from the fitted shape. Results in `tests/autofit_results.json`.

## 1.3.0

- **Large generic pose library.** Besides the humanoid core probes, every movable deform bone (structurally chosen: has a parent, weights the body, is not a global control) is bent about its rest-local X and Z axes (±60°) and twisted (±35°), and each such bone bends together with each movable child (±45° each). Core probes are also sampled at intermediate angles. No garment names, body regions or avatar proportions are assumed.
- **Proven pose exclusion.** A whole motion line is skipped only when it moves no garment candidate bone and its moving body vertices stay inside a ball around the rotating joint that is out of collision range of the static garment. A single pose reuses Rest (or another evaluated pose) only when every garment candidate transform and correspondence target is identical and every moved body triangle is provably out of range at both positions. Preserve Volume disables both proofs.
- **Adaptive joint-angle sampling.** Lines start at their full angle; a midpoint is added when a multi-joint line shows a problem or the moving body may sweep through range between samples, and intervals are bisected further where a problem disappears or severity peaks (non-monotonic failures). Thorough mode samples six angles per line and bisects to 1/24.
- **Separate screening from validation.** Library poses are screened without BVH queries by exact linear-blend strain/collapse/fold metrics, an own-surface penetration estimate and a moving-body intrusion count (reported as `linear prediction`). Automatic mode promotes to Blender-deformation validation only library poses that reveal a problem kind or severity no core pose shows; Thorough validates every relevant library pose. Refinement targets validated core poses ranked by severity; screened poses guard acceptance once, and regions they predict worse return to the initial transfer before a final check.
- **Incremental, exact validation.** Collision results are reused for points whose actual position did not change and that no moved body triangle can reach; self-intersections are retested only around changed triangles. Collision BVHs hold only body triangles that can lie within the search range of the queried points (exact up to equal-distance tie-breaks) and skip provably distant points.
- **Localized refinement.** Only vertices with pose-added clipping, crossings, collapsed/folded faces or large body-following error (3% of avatar size), grown geodesically, are updated; weights outside a smoothing halo keep the exact initial transfer. Passes stop early when weights stop moving or the collision/crossing residual stalls; separating forces are computed only for poses that had new crossings.
- **Batched rig synchronization.** Independent clothing rigs are posed analytically with `Bone.convert_local_to_pose` (parent-aware, honoring inheritance options) and evaluated with one dependency-graph update per pose instead of one per linked bone; matrices match the previous sequential assignment within 1e-6.
- **Vectorized self-intersection tests** (float64 Möller–Trumbore on all candidate pairs, budget raised to 400k pairs). Every pose is measured on the garment's rest triangulation, so crossing indices stay consistent when Blender re-triangulates n-gons in a pose (1.2.1 subtracted rest crossings from per-pose triangulation indices).
- Linear-blend body prediction follows Blender's Armature normalization, including vertices whose raw deform weight is below Blender's 1e-4 threshold (they stay at rest).
- **Profiling.** The report adds `profile` (inclusive seconds per operation: depsgraph updates, pose sync, mesh extraction, BVH builds/queries, self-intersections, screening, refinement steps, actual validation; counters; per-pose seconds), `pose_library` (generated, proven equivalent, screened, promoted, validated and refined poses, refinement iterations, linear-prediction error) and `screened_poses`/`promoted_poses`. `phase_seconds` adds `pose_screening`.
- New **Pose validation** setting: Automatic (default) or Thorough.
- Add `tests/pose_sync_regression.py`, `tests/performance_benchmark.py`, `tests/benchmark_scenes.py` and `tests/run_benchmarks.py`; `tests/pose_runtime_regression.py` now checks broad-phase conservativeness, exclusion proofs and that incremental validation equals a full recomputation.
- Measured against 1.2.1 (Blender 5.0.1, defaults, same independent evaluator): supplied pants 107.95 s → 61.70 s (1.75×, 867 library poses screened, final weights identical), supplied hoodie 127.87 s → 98.09 s (1.30×, 1506 library poses), five synthetic garments 1.21–1.38× faster; a secondary-bone top 0.94× and a 252-vertex hat 0.61× (fixed screening cost). Evaluated quality changed by less than ±1.5% on every metric; the secondary-bone top now keeps the initial transfer because promoted library poses failed the aggregate clearance check. See README and `tests/benchmark/summary.json`.

## 1.1.0

- Choose body correspondence by confidence and neighborhood consistency. Each garment vertex keeps up to four distinct nearby body surfaces; confidence combines distance, orientation-agnostic facing (double-sided and folded fabric is not penalized) and the weight disagreement of equally close surfaces. Ambiguous vertices are re-assigned only for a clear gain against their confident neighbors' opinion (leave-one-out consensus with hysteresis), e.g. an inner sleeve closer to the torso than to the arm.
- Replace uniform edge averaging with a geometry-aware, confidence-weighted screened smoothing solve (clamped cotangent weights with lumped areas; 1D stiffness for loose edges; separate islands stay independent). Reliable contacts keep their body weights; loose, distant or ambiguous fabric is inpainted from its reliable surroundings. Skin-tight fabric is blurred no wider than its body offset so it still follows concave skin creases. New **Transition smoothing (% avatar size)** setting replaces the dimensionless Weight smoothing factor; 0 keeps raw nearest-surface weights.
- Propagate candidate bone support over the garment's geodesic neighborhood before influence pruning, so adjacent vertices with initially disjoint bone sets can blend and refinement updates can cross between them.
- Couple stacked garment layers (pockets, linings, cords, solidified thickness, folds, a hood lying on the back) found by normal rays when both follow the same body region. Coupled layers share smoothing, refinement updates and rest-fit displacement so they keep moving together; a sleeve against the torso (opposing body normals) is not merged.
